# Microwave Bread — Misconception Map

> Version: 0.1
> Purpose: Identify likely incomplete physical models and design interactions that help students revise them.

## M01 — Heat as a Substance Stored in an Object

**Possible student idea:**
“Bread has more heat inside it after microwaving.”

**Risk:**
The student may treat heat as a substance that an object stores in the same sense as mass.

**Target direction:**
Use energy-transfer language carefully. Avoid treating heat as a material substance.

**Useful intervention:**
Ask:
“What changed in the bread, and how do you know?”

---

## M02 — Absorbing Energy Always Means Temperature Must Increase

**Possible student idea:**
“If an object absorbs energy, its temperature must rise.”

**Risk:**
The student may not understand that energy can contribute to changes other than immediate temperature increase, such as phase change.

**Target direction:**
Introduce a counterexample only when pedagogically appropriate.

**Useful intervention:**
Use melting ice as a later contrast.

---

## M03 — Higher Temperature Always Means Greater Internal Energy

**Possible student idea:**
“A 100°C object always has more internal energy than a 50°C object.”

**Risk:**
The student ignores mass, material, and system definition.

**Target direction:**
Teach that a single quantity cannot automatically determine another quantity without conditions.

**Useful intervention:**
Compare different masses/materials.

---

## M04 — All Heating Is the Same Process

**Possible student idea:**
“Everything that makes something hot is just heat transfer in the same way.”

**Risk:**
The student focuses only on the result “hot” and ignores the process.

**Target direction:**
Compare microwave heating, hot-water contact, and rubbing hands.

**Useful intervention:**
Ask:
“What is different about how energy reaches the object?”

---

## M05 — Naming a Concept Means Understanding It

**Possible student idea:**
“This is internal energy.”

But the student cannot explain:
- what changed;
- why it changed;
- how it predicts a new situation.

**Target direction:**
Treat terminology as evidence of vocabulary, not proof of model formation.

**Useful intervention:**
Always follow terminology with:
“Why?” or “How would you know?”

---

## M06 — Temperature, Heat, and Internal Energy Are Interchangeable

**Possible student idea:**
“Temperature increased because heat increased.”

**Target direction:**
Explicitly distinguish:
- temperature;
- heat/energy transfer;
- internal energy.

**Useful intervention:**
Use comparison questions and exam distractors.

---

## M07 — A Correct Answer Means the Model Is Correct

**Possible student behavior:**
Student chooses the correct option but cannot explain why.

**Target direction:**
Separate:
- answer correctness;
- reasoning quality;
- model evidence.

**Intervention:**
Ask for a short explanation after selected answers.

---

## M08 — A Familiar Surface Context Means the Same Solution Method

**Possible student behavior:**
Student succeeds on microwave bread but cannot explain a hot-water bag.

**Target direction:**
Train structural transfer rather than surface imitation.

**Intervention:**
Use multiple scenarios sharing the underlying model.

---

## M09 — Temperature Change Identifies the Energy Transfer Mechanism Automatically

**Possible student idea:**
“If temperature increased, heat transfer must have occurred.”

**Target direction:**
Separate the outcome from the process that caused the outcome.

**Intervention:**
Compare warming by contact with warming caused by doing work.

---

## M10 — Advanced Microphysics Is Required to Explain the Basic Phenomenon

**Possible student behavior:**
Student thinks the explanation is impossible without understanding detailed microwave molecular physics.

**Target direction:**
Teach the right level of model for the task.

**Intervention:**
Use the simplest adequate model for Grade 9:
energy transfer → internal state change → temperature change.
